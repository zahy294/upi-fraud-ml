## Q3. P1: Indistinguishable Positives at Row Level (58% Camouflaged / Entity-Level Fraud)
Kaggle IEEE-CIS discussion threads proved that when ~50%+ of positive rows look identical to legit rows, **labels were generated at the entity/account/episode level** (all transactions of a compromised account/card or episode get converted to positive), and top teams recovered massive gains via **entity-group mean/max post-processing** and **camouflage-resistant graph filtering**.

Therefore we are predicting fraudulent clients (credit cards)." | **Yes (20 min):** Check in train what share of the 58% indistinguishable fraud rows share a `sender_id`, `receiver_id`, `device_id`, or `(sender_id, 24h_window)` with an obvious fraud row. |

## Q5. P3: Test Month Distribution Shift (Volume -50%, Amount +16.5%, Broken Features)
Kaggle IEEE-CIS discussions and covariate-shift literature show that when test volume and amounts shift and features break, **dropping high-adversarial-gain features**, **normalizing amounts/counts per day or per cohort**, and **importance weighting** recover LB performance.

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/ieee-fraud-detection/discussion/109944 | 2019 | Used adversarial validation (train=0, test=1) to identify time-drifting/broken features and normalized counts and amounts against the local period / cohort. | "The reason adversarial validation has AUC=1 is not because the nature of fraud changes radically over time but rather because the clients in the dataset change radically over time. Once you realize this, you will know that the challenge in this competition is building a model that can predict unseen clients (not unseen time)." | **Yes (45 min):** Drop `app_version` and `ip_risk_score` (or train a clean model without them and blend); divide October `amount` by `1.165` (or use `amount / daily_median_amount`) before computing `amount / balance_before`, and multiply October rolling window counts (`txns_1h`, `fan_in_24h`) by `2.0` to correct the -50% volume shift. |

## Q6. P2: Typing Lookalike Classes & Optimising Macro-F1 Under Confusion
Kaggle discussions, Reddit threads, and hierarchical classification papers show that when lookalike classes overlap, flat `argmax` fails; **coarse-to-fine specialist models** and **out-of-fold Nelder-Mead multiplier/cost-matrix optimization** yield large Macro-F1 gains.

## Top 5 Ideas Most Worth Testing Tonight (With Exact First Experiment)

1. **Entity/Episode Max-Score Propagation for the 58% Indistinguishable Rows (Targets P1, +0.03–0.06 Macro-F1)**
   * **Why:** As shown in Kaggle's IEEE-CIS discussions (`discussion/111284` & `111307`), when 58% of fraud rows are statistically identical to `legit` rows on every column, the generator labelled *entire compromised accounts/devices or episodes* as fraud (so normal background transactions of that entity inherit the fraud label).
   * **Exact First Experiment:** Group train by `sender_id`, `receiver_id`, and `device_id`. Check what fraction of the 58% indistinguishable fraud rows share an entity ID (or `(sender_id, 48h_window)`) with a high-confidence fraud row. On OOF predictions, test `P_final(c | row i) = (1 - a) * P(c | row i) + a * max_{j in same entity} P(c | row j)` across `a in [0.2, 0.4, 0.6]` and measure OOF Macro-F1.

2. **October Shift Correction: Scale-Adjusted Recipes + Drop Broken Columns (Targets P3, +0.02–0.04 LB)**
   * **Why:** October has 50% fewer rows/day and +16.5% higher legit amounts while balances are unchanged, and `app_version` + `ip_risk_score` stopped working. Any tree split on raw `amount`, `amount / balance_before`, or rolling time-window counts (`txns_last_1h`) is systematically miscalibrated in October.
   * **Exact First Experiment:** (a) Train a model completely dropping `app_version` and `ip_risk_score` (or set them to `NaN` at inference) and blend with your main model. (b) Divide October `amount` by `1.165` (or normalize `amount / daily_median_amount` per day across both train and test) *before* computing your exact fraud recipes and `amount / balance_before`, and multiply October rolling time-window transaction counts/velocities by `2.0`.

3. **4 Specialist Fine-Grained Classifiers + Event-Pooled Features on the Confused Super-Clusters (Targets P2 & P4, +0.02–0.04 Macro-F1)**
   * **Why:** Your 4 lookalike groups (`sim_swap/takeover`, `mule/layering/bot`, `phishing/cashback/loan`, `qr/refund/collusion`) are generated in each other's row shape ~25% of the time, but multiple transactions belonging to the same *event/entity* contain the disambiguating cues.
   * **Exact First Experiment:** Train 4 dedicated LightGBM classifiers restricted to high-confidence clean rows of each super-cluster. Feed both row features and **entity-level min/max/std features** (e.g., `min(sim_age_hours)`, `max(pin_retries)`, `min(login_to_txn_sec)`, `max(qr_vpa_mismatch)`, hop depth, `inflow / outflow` ratio across all txns of that `sender_id`/`receiver_id`). Combine via `P(fine_class) = P_coarse(super_cluster) * P_specialist(fine_class | super_cluster)`.

4. **Full-Precision Float64 Noise-Floor & High-`max_bin` Generator Audit (Targets Q2 & P2, 20 mins)**
   * **Why:** As documented in Kaggle's Amex (`ambrosm`/`raddar`) and Playground Series discussions, synthetic generators often add uniform `[0, 0.01)` noise to integer-quantized rules or use mode-specific cutoffs that LightGBM's default `max_bin=255` blurs.
   * **Exact First Experiment:** Inspect `amount`, `balance_before`, `sim_age_hours`, `login_to_txn_sec`, and `ip_risk_score` at full `float64` precision (`x * 100 - np.floor(x * 100)` and `(x * 100) % 10`) across the 11 fraud classes, and retrain your LightGBM with `max_bin=1023` + frequency encoding on rounded numeric columns.

5. **Out-of-Fold Nelder-Mead Confusion-Matrix & Prior-Aligned Decoding (Targets P2 & P3, 20 mins)**
   * **Why:** Per-class weights alone cannot resolve symmetric tie-breaking between two lookalike classes or adjust for October's shifted volume.
   * **Exact First Experiment:** Use `scipy.optimize.minimize(..., method='Nelder-Mead')` with 5 random restarts on OOF probabilities to jointly optimize 12 class multipliers $w_c$ and a $4\times 1$ tie-breaker margin for the 4 confused pairs, constrained so the predicted October class distribution matches your clean fraud-type prior ratios.
